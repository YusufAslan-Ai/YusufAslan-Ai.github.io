How to use Google Search Console for e-commerce

By Yusuf Aslan · 7 min read · November 2026

Google Search Console is Google’s free tool that shows how your site appears in Google Search. For an online store it does three jobs: it shows which queries and pages bring traffic (Performance report), whether your important product and category pages are indexed (Page indexing report), and where your product results have errors (Shopping reports). Below is the order I use, from setup to a weekly routine, based on Google’s own help pages.

Which property type should you choose?

You start by adding your site as a “property”. There are two kinds: a Domain property and a URL-prefix property. According to Google’s guide to adding a property, a Domain property covers every subdomain and both http and https, but it can only be verified with a DNS record. A URL-prefix property covers one protocol and path and offers many verification methods.

Domain propertyURL-prefix property
ScopeAll subdomains, http and httpsOnly the exact address you enter and paths under it
VerificationDNS record onlyHTML file, meta tag, Google Analytics, Tag Manager and more
When to useFirst choice if you can edit DNSIf you can’t access DNS or only want to track one folder

For online stores I always start with a Domain property so that www, non-www and mobile subdomain traffic aren’t split across separate properties. On platforms like Shopify, the DNS usually sits with your domain registrar, so that’s where you add the TXT record.

Which metrics matter in the Performance report?

The Performance report shows four metrics: clicks (how often people clicked through to your site from Google), impressions (how often your site appeared in results), CTR (clicks divided by impressions) and average position (the average position of your topmost result). The default view covers the last three months. The newest data can be preliminary and may change within hours; it is shown as a dotted line.

Here is how I read it for a store:

  • Queries tab: high impressions with low CTR point to pages whose title and description need work.
  • Pages tab: see whether traffic lands on product pages, category pages or blog posts. In most stores the biggest opportunity is category pages; I cover how to strengthen them in category page SEO for e-commerce.
  • Compare: choose “Compare” in the date filter, put two periods side by side and sort by clicks difference.

One thing to know: Google’s notes on the data explain that by property, all results from your site for the same query count once, while by page each URL counts separately. So CTR and position in the Pages tab can differ from the property total. That’s expected, not a bug.

Why aren’t your product pages indexed?

The Page indexing report lists which pages Google has indexed and why others aren’t. Google is clear that most sites have some unindexed pages and that you shouldn’t expect every URL to be indexed; what matters is that your important pages are. The reasons I see most in e-commerce:

  • Crawled – currently not indexed: Google saw the page but didn’t add it. Common when product copy is taken from the manufacturer, very thin, or nearly identical to other pages.
  • Discovered – currently not indexed: Google knows the URL but hasn’t crawled it yet. Often a sign of weak internal linking or too many low-value URLs.
  • Duplicate without user-selected canonical: filter, sort and variant parameter URLs repeating the same content.

Don’t try to fix everything in the list. Ask first: should this page bring sales? Fix the product and category pages that should, and leave the rest out of the index on purpose. If the 404 count jumps right after you change platforms, missing redirects are the usual cause; I cover the checks in my platform migration SEO checklist.

How do you tell Google about a new page?

For a single page, use the URL Inspection tool:

  1. Paste the full URL into the search bar at the top of Search Console.
  2. Check whether it’s indexed, which canonical Google chose and when it was last crawled.
  3. If you changed the page, run “Test live URL” to see how Google sees it now.
  4. If everything looks fine, click “Request indexing”.
  5. For many pages, don’t request one by one; submit your sitemap under “Sitemaps”.

Google notes there is a daily request limit per property and that a request doesn’t guarantee indexing. Requesting the same page every day won’t help; the issue is usually the page content or site structure.

What do the shopping reports do for a store?

According to Google’s shopping reports page, if Google identifies your site as an online merchant you get extra tools: the Merchant listings and Product snippets rich result reports, a merchant opportunities report under “Shopping”, and shipping and returns settings. These reports flag missing price, availability or review fields in your product structured data. I explain how to set that data up in structured data (schema) for product pages.

Why use annotations?

In November 2025 Google added custom annotations to the Performance report. You can attach a note of up to 120 characters to a date on the chart, and everyone with access to the property can see it. I log every site change, sale period and theme update there, so when traffic shifts you don’t have to guess why. It’s especially useful for separating a ranking drop after a Google core update from changes you made yourself.

What should a weekly routine look like?

You don’t need to check daily. Once a week, 15 minutes in this order is enough:

  1. In Performance, compare the last 7 days with the previous 7; review the 5 pages that lost the most clicks.
  2. In Page indexing, look for a sudden jump in “not indexed” pages.
  3. Check the shopping and rich result reports for new errors.
  4. Add that week’s changes as annotations.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Search Console free?

Yes. Google Search Console is completely free; you only need to verify that you own the site.

Should I choose a Domain or a URL-prefix property?

If you can edit DNS, choose a Domain property; it covers all subdomains and both http and https. If you can’t access DNS, use a URL-prefix property.

What does “Crawled – currently not indexed” mean?

Google crawled the page but didn’t index it. Many sites have some pages in this state; if it’s an important product or category page, make the content unique and strengthen internal links.

Should I click Request indexing every day?

No. Google applies a daily request limit per property and a request doesn’t guarantee indexing. For many pages, submitting a sitemap is the better route.

How fresh is Search Console data?

The most recent day can be preliminary and may change within hours. By default the report shows complete days.
