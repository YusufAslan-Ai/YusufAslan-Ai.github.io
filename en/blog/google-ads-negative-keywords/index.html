How negative keywords protect your Google Ads budget

By Yusuf Aslan · 4 min read · October 2026

What are negative keywords?

They are words that define which searches you do not want your ad to show for. Searches such as "free", "how to" or "second hand" bring clicks but not sales.

Look at the search terms report

This report shows which searches actually produced clicks. You find the ones that spent money without sales and add them to the list. Doing this at least twice a month eases the budget in most accounts. During big sales irrelevant searches grow, so check it daily; the full list is in Google Ads Black Friday preparation.

Mind the match types

A very broad negative can also cut off searches that sell. Before adding one, think about which searches it will block; if unsure, use a narrower match type.

Build account-level lists

Instead of adding to each campaign one by one, create a shared negative list. When new campaigns launch you do not repeat the same mistake.

Frequently asked questions

What is a negative keyword?

A word that tells Google which searches your ad should not show for. Searches like "free", "how to" or "second hand" bring clicks but usually not sales.

How do I find which negative keywords to add?

Use the search terms report. It shows which searches your clicks actually came from, so you can spot the ones that spend money without selling and add them to the list.

How often should I update my negative keyword list?

Checking the search terms report at least twice a month relieves the budget in most accounts. A shared list at account level, rather than per campaign, makes this easier.
