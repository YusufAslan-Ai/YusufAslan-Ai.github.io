Switching e-commerce platforms without losing SEO

By Yusuf Aslan · 8 min read · November 2026

To switch e-commerce platforms (say, from IdeaSoft to ikas or from Ticimax to Shopify) without losing SEO, you connect every important URL on the old site to its match on the new one with a 301 permanent redirect. Google says permanent redirects don’t cause a loss in PageRank, but product and category pages whose redirects are forgotten return 404s and lose their traffic. Expect some temporary ranking movement for a few weeks after the move. Below is the order I follow when planning a migration.

Once the November sales are over, many store owners decide to start the new year on a new platform. The decision can be right; the risk is in how the move is carried out. A product page that has ranked for years and is moved to a new address, with the old one left empty, simply disappears from Google’s view overnight.

Why does changing platforms affect SEO?

Because each platform builds URLs with its own pattern. On Shopify, for example, product pages start with /products/ and category pages with /collections/, and those prefixes can’t be changed. If your old platform used a different structure, almost every product and category URL changes even when the domain stays the same. To Google, that is a site move with URL changes.

Google’s site move guide is clear: it recommends permanent redirects (301 or 308) and says permanent redirects don’t cause a loss in PageRank. Done right, the trust your old pages earned passes to the new ones. If you haven’t picked a platform yet, I compare payments, marketplace integrations and costs in my ikas vs Shopify platform comparison.

What should you prepare before the move?

The key piece of prep is a URL inventory of the old site. Build it from three sources: the old sitemap.xml, pages with clicks in the last 12 months in Search Console’s Performance report, and pages with external links. I show how to pull that list in my Google Search Console guide for online stores.

Then decide what to do with each page type:

Old pageMatch on the new site?What to do
Product pageYes301 to the exact new product URL
Category pageYes301 to the exact new category URL
Discontinued productNo, but a similar product exists301 to the closest product or its category
Content removed for goodNoReturn 404 or 410 (Google’s advice)
Blog post, static pageYes301 one-to-one; move the content too

How do you switch platforms without losing SEO? 8 steps

  1. Build the URL inventory. Merge the three sources into one sheet and flag the top 100–200 traffic pages; you’ll check those first.
  2. Create a mapping sheet. Write the new URL next to each old one. Don’t send everything to the homepage; Google recommends a 404 or 410 for removed content, and an unrelated destination confuses shoppers too.
  3. Set up 301 redirects on the new platform. Most platforms support one-by-one or bulk CSV redirects. According to Shopify’s URL redirects help page, you add them under Content > Menus > URL redirects, can import them by CSV, and a redirect only works from a URL that no longer loads a page in the store. ikas’s redirect guide has you add them in the SEO & Domain settings, enter only the path after the domain (such as /contact) and set them up before you connect your domain. Whichever platform you move to, check the current menu path in its own docs.
  4. Avoid redirect chains. Don’t let old URL → interim URL → new URL chains build up. Google advises redirecting straight to the final destination, so update any redirects from the old platform to point at the new final URLs.
  5. Carry over on-page SEO in full. Title tags, meta descriptions, product descriptions and image alt text should come across unchanged. Category intro texts are the content most often lost in a move; I explain why they matter in my category page SEO article. Every new page needs a self-referencing canonical tag, and you should confirm that price, stock and review data still render in the new theme using the checks in my product page structured data guide.
  6. Update internal links and menus. Menus, banners, links inside blog posts and the footer should point to the new URLs. Google specifically recommends changing internal links from old to new URLs; an internal link that passes through a redirect is a wasted step.
  7. Remove blocks at launch and submit the sitemap. Remove any noindex tags and robots.txt blocks added during testing, then submit the new sitemap in Search Console; Google says this can speed up discovery of the new URLs. If your domain isn’t changing, you don’t need the Change of Address tool; Google only uses it for moves from one domain to another.
  8. Reconnect tracking and ad feeds. Analytics tags, Google Ads conversions, the Meta Pixel and Conversions API, and your Merchant Center product feed are tied to the platform and reset in a move. Test the purchase event on launch day; I cover the setup in my Pixel and Conversions API guide. If changed product links trigger disapprovals, see the common Merchant Center disapproval causes.

What should you monitor after the move?

On day one, check your top 100–200 URLs from the inventory with a crawler or a simple script: each should go to the right new page in a single 301 hop. Over the following weeks, watch the “Not found (404)” count in Search Console’s Page indexing report; a sudden jump usually means missing redirects.

Be patient. Google’s guide says to expect temporary ranking fluctuation during a move, and for medium-sized sites it can take a few weeks or more before the new URLs start showing. If a traffic drop lands in the same week as the move, check the dates covered in my Google core update article so you don’t confuse the two. Don’t rush to remove redirects either: Google recommends keeping them as long as possible, generally at least 1 year.

What are the most common mistakes?

  • Migrating during a peak sales period. A tracking or redirect error in a campaign week is the most expensive kind. Pick a quiet week.
  • Shutting down the old site immediately. Don’t cancel the old subscription before exporting product descriptions, the URL list and existing redirects.
  • Moving only the products. Category texts, blog posts and static pages may bring traffic too.
  • Leaving the staging site indexable. If the new site appears in Google before launch, it creates duplicate content with the old one.

Frequently asked questions

Will my Google rankings drop if I change platforms?

Some temporary movement is normal. Google says to expect ranking fluctuation during a move and that for medium-sized sites it can take a few weeks or more for the new URLs to show. If every important URL is 301-redirected to the right page, you shouldn’t expect a lasting loss.

How long should I keep 301 redirects?

Google recommends keeping redirects as long as possible, generally at least 1 year. Keep them longer if the old URLs still get traffic or links.

Can I redirect all old pages to the homepage?

It isn’t recommended. Each old URL should go to the closest new page by content. For content removed with no replacement, Google recommends returning a 404 or 410.

Do I need the Change of Address tool if my domain stays the same?

No. Search Console’s Change of Address tool is only for moves from one domain to another. If only the URL structure changes, 301 redirects and a new sitemap are enough.

When should I set up the redirects?

Before the new site goes live and before you connect your domain. ikas also recommends setting redirects up before connecting the domain, so old URLs land on the right pages the moment you launch.
