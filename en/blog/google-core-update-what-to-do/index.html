What is a Google core update? What to do if rankings drop

By Yusuf Aslan · 7 min read · November 2026

A Google core update is a broad change to Google’s ranking systems that happens several times a year; it doesn’t punish specific sites, it reassesses content across the web. If your rankings dropped, don’t panic: wait for the update to finish, compare periods in Search Console at least a week after it completes, and if the drop is large, review your pages by asking whether they are truly helpful to people.

I’m writing this while the November 2026 core update is still rolling out. Google started it on November 10, and according to the Search Status Dashboard entry it applies globally and to all languages; it may take up to two weeks to complete. That puts it right in Black Friday week, the most sensitive time for e-commerce. For the paid side, see Google Ads preparation for Black Friday; this article is about organic search.

What does a Google core update actually change?

According to Google’s guide to core updates, they are “significant, broad changes” to its search algorithms and systems, and they don’t target specific sites or pages. So a drop doesn’t necessarily mean something is broken on your site; Google may simply have found other pages that serve the same query better.

Naming the cause correctly decides what you do next. Google’s guide to debugging traffic drops lists technical issues, security problems, spam violations, seasonality and site moves alongside algorithm updates:

CauseWhat it looks likeWhere to look first
Core updateDrops that line up with update dates and vary by pageSearch Status Dashboard, Performance report
Technical issueSudden, sharp, site-wide dropPages report (noindex, 404, robots.txt)
Spam violationPages disappearing from resultsSearch Console > Manual actions
SeasonalityDrops in the same months every year16-month view in the Performance report
Site moveFluctuation after URL changes301 redirects from old URLs

What do we know about the November 2026 core update?

  • It started on November 10, 2026; Google says it “may take up to 2 weeks to complete.”
  • Google called it a regular update to better surface relevant, satisfying content from all types of sites (Search Engine Roundtable).
  • It’s the third core update of 2026: the March update ran 12 days and the May update about 12 days (PPC Land).
  • Google hasn’t said what it targets. Be cautious with takes claiming a specific signal changed.

Rankings dropped? What to do in 6 steps

  1. Confirm the update has finished. Wait until the Search Status Dashboard marks it complete; rankings swing while it rolls out.
  2. Wait at least a full week after completion. Google recommends not analyzing before then.
  3. Compare the right periods. In the Search Console Performance report, compare the week after the update with the week before it started. Sort the pages table by clicks difference to see which pages lost the most. If you’re new to these reports, start with my guide on how to use Google Search Console for e-commerce.
  4. Size the drop. Google’s own example: falling from position 2 to 4 needs no drastic action; falling from 4 to 29 calls for a deeper assessment.
  5. Rule out technical and seasonal causes. If impressions and clicks both fell, check indexing, noindex and 404 errors; if only clicks fell, review your titles and descriptions. Use the 16-month view to compare with last year.
  6. Compare losing pages with the ones that replaced them. What do the pages now ranking above you answer better? Rewrite your content meaningfully based on that.

Which pages should an online store check first?

Most store traffic comes from three places: category pages, product pages and blog posts. Broad searches like “women’s boots” usually land on categories, so start there; thin categories that are just a product grid are fragile in updates like this. I list what’s usually missing in SEO for e-commerce category pages.

On product pages, the most common issue is supplier copy that appears word for word on dozens of sites. Ask every important page the self-assessment questions from Google’s helpful content guide:

  • Does the content provide original information, research or analysis?
  • Would a visitor want to bookmark it or recommend it to a friend?
  • Was it written or reviewed by someone who knows the topic well?
  • Does it contain easily verified factual errors?

The guide also stresses “who, how and why”: make it clear who wrote the content and how it was made, and make serving visitors the main purpose, not search traffic. On product pages, make sure price, stock and review data reach Google correctly too; don’t skip checking structured data (schema) on product pages.

What should you avoid after a core update?

  • Chasing quick fixes. Google explicitly advises against superficial changes made only because you heard they help SEO.
  • Mass-deleting content. Google calls removal a last resort; try improving first.
  • Changing things daily during the rollout. Decisions made mid-fluctuation rest on noisy data. If you spot a real technical error (noindex, 404), fix it right away, of course.
  • Rushing a URL restructure. A new migration adds a second wave of fluctuation on top of the drop.

How long does recovery take?

Google says some improvements can show effects within days, but it may take months for its systems to confirm a site consistently produces helpful content. If nothing changes after several months, you may need to wait for the next core update. Because Google also runs smaller, unannounced updates in between, improvements can pay off before then, though Google guarantees no specific outcome.

Use that time well. The same quality signals affect visibility in AI answers, so the points in Google AI Mode for e-commerce and the 10-point SEO and GEO checklist fit straight into a recovery plan.

How do you protect sales while organic traffic is down?

Recovery can take weeks or months, but your promotional calendar won’t wait. It makes sense to balance sales with paid channels in the meantime: push Google Shopping campaigns and Meta catalog ads for the categories that dropped. Frequently asked questions

Is a Google core update a penalty?

No. Google says core updates don’t target specific sites or pages; its systems reassess content across the web. Penalty-like situations show up in the Manual actions report in Search Console.

How long does a Google core update take?

Usually about two weeks. The November 2026 update started on November 10 and Google said it may take up to two weeks; the March and May 2026 updates each took about 12 days.

When will my rankings come back after a core update?

There’s no fixed timeline. Google says some improvements can show effects within days, but confirming them can take months. Sometimes you have to wait for the next core update.

Should I change my site while the update is rolling out?

For analysis, wait until it finishes plus at least one more week. If you see a real technical issue such as noindex, 404s or server errors, fix it right away; make content decisions once the data settles.
