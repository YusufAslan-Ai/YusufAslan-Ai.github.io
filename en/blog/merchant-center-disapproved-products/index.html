Merchant Center disapproved products: 7 causes and fixes

By Yusuf Aslan · 7 min read · November 2026

If a product is disapproved in Merchant Center, it stops showing anywhere on Google: it drops out of Shopping ads, Performance Max and free listings. The most common causes are a price or availability in the feed that doesn’t match the product page, images that are too small or carry promotional text, missing GTIN and brand data, and gaps in contact, return or payment information. The fix is to find the issue in the “Needs attention” tab under Products, correct it at the source and request a review where needed. Below I go through these seven causes and how to fix each one, based on Google’s own help pages.

Shopping campaigns and Performance Max run on the same product feed. Whichever you choose, a disapproved product won’t serve; I compare the two in Performance Max versus Shopping campaigns for e-commerce. This article is the guide to cleaning up the feed underneath them.

What is the difference between a warning, a disapproval and a suspension?

Not every Merchant Center issue carries the same weight. Based on Google’s page on issues in Merchant Center:

StatusWhat happensWhat to do
WarningThe product keeps showing, but performance may be limitedFix it while you can; unresolved warnings can turn into disapprovals
DisapprovalThe product stops showing across GoogleCorrect the product data and resubmit
Pre-emptive item disapprovalProducts are disapproved as a precaution because price or availability doesn’t match the product pageFix the mismatch and request a review
Account suspensionSetup and policy issues not fixed within the warning period stop all products; egregious cases can happen without warningFix the issue on your site and use “Request review”

Where do you find disapproved products?

In Merchant Center, open Products and go to the Needs attention tab. Use the filter icon to narrow issues by impact, label or title, and the download icon to export every affected item as a .csv file. Each issue has its own details page showing how many products are affected and Google’s suggested fix.

My advice: sort by the number of affected products and start with the issue that hits the most. Very often one theme or integration setting is disapproving hundreds of products at once.

What are the 7 most common reasons products get disapproved?

1. Does the price or availability match the product page?

This is the most common one. Google wants the price in your feed to match the product page and checkout exactly. If the feed says 899 TL and the site says 949 TL, or the feed says “in stock” while the page says “sold out”, products get disapproved pre-emptively. It usually happens during sales: the site price changes, but the feed only updates once a day. I explain how to add dated sale prices in Google Ads Black Friday preparation.

Fix: Update your feed more often and show the same price in the product page’s structured data, which Google also reads when it crawls. See the guide to structured data on product pages to set up the markup correctly.

2. Does your price include VAT?

According to Google’s price [price] attribute page, outside the US and Canada you should submit prices including VAT. Some platforms push VAT-exclusive prices to the feed, which creates a mismatch with the page. The currency must match the page too: TRY if you target Türkiye.

3. Is the image too small or covered in text?

According to Google’s “Image too small” page, today’s minimum is 100 x 100 pixels for non-apparel and 250 x 250 for apparel, and files can’t exceed 64 megapixels or 16 MB. One important note: the same page says a 500 x 500 pixel minimum for all product images will be enforced from 31 January 2027. Small images may only get a warning today, but that changes after this date.

Content matters as much as size. Google’s page on product data quality lists generic and promotional images among common issues, so a “30% off” overlay, a watermark or a logo stamped on the photo is risky. The rules for clean product images are the same on marketplaces; the checklist in marketplace product images and descriptions works for Merchant Center too.

4. Is the GTIN or brand missing or wrong?

Submitting a product that has a barcode (GTIN) without it, or using another product’s barcode, is a frequent mistake. For your own, handmade or one-of-a-kind products, leave GTIN empty and submit identifier_exists as no. Careful: if the product does have a barcode, setting this to no leads to a warning. Don’t apply it to the whole catalogue just to make errors disappear.

5. Are required attributes missing?

Some categories need extra fields. According to Google, apparel and accessories require gender, size, colour and age group. If your platform doesn’t pull these from variants automatically, fill them in with feed rules or a supplemental data source. An invalid Google product category is on the same list.

6. Does the title use excessive capitals or filler?

Titles like “NEW SEASON WOMEN’S DRESS !!! FREE SHIPPING” cause issues because of excessive capitalisation. Write brand, product type and the distinguishing attribute in a plain order. The logic I use for marketplaces works for Google feeds too; details in how to write a marketplace product title.

7. Is something on your site treated as misrepresentation?

This is an account-level issue, not a product one, and the most frustrating. Google’s misrepresentation policy prohibits a false business name, address or contact information, not disclosing the total price including taxes and fees, and refusing returns despite a clear return policy. It requires at least one conventional payment method at checkout, such as credit card, debit card or payment on delivery. Showing delivery time prominently while hiding handling time in small print is also not allowed.

Fix: Make sure your site has an easy-to-find contact page (phone, email, address) and clear return and shipping policy pages, and that they match your Merchant Center settings.

What should you do after fixing the issue?

  1. Fix it at the source. Correct the product data in your platform or the feed rule rather than editing the item in Merchant Center; otherwise the error returns with the next update.
  2. Resubmit the feed. For product-level issues, the disapproval disappears once the review succeeds.
  3. Request a review for policy and setup issues. Use the “Request review” button in the Needs attention tab. Google says these reviews can take up to seven days.
  4. Don’t waste requests. Google notes there is a limit on how many reviews you can request, so don’t ask before the issue is fully fixed.
  5. Watch the warning period. If you don’t request a review during it, Google reviews your data at the end; if the issue remains, the account is suspended.

How do you prevent disapprovals in the first place?

Check the Needs attention tab weekly, test how fast prices update before every sale, and make image and GTIN checks part of adding a new product. Frequently asked questions

Do disapproved Merchant Center products still show in ads?

No. According to Google, disapproved products stop showing across Google, so they drop out of Shopping ads, Performance Max and free listings. Products with a warning keep showing, but their performance may be limited.

How long does it take for a fixed product to be approved again?

For product-level issues, you fix and resubmit the data, and the disapproval disappears if the review succeeds. For setup and policy issues, you use “Request review”; Google says these reviews can take up to seven days.

My handmade products have no GTIN. What should I do?

Leave the GTIN and MPN fields empty and submit identifier_exists as no. Google recommends this for custom, handmade and one-of-a-kind items. If the product actually has a barcode, setting this to no leads to a warning.

Can a price mismatch get my account suspended?

With price and availability mismatches, Google first disapproves the affected products as a precaution. This is called pre-emptive item disapproval and needs a review to be resolved. Leaving it unfixed alongside other policy issues raises the risk of account-level action.
