Meta partnership ads: what they are and how to set them up

By Yusuf Aslan · 7 min read · November 2026

Meta partnership ads are ads that run a creator’s or partner’s content under their handle together with yours, from your own ad account and on your own budget. Both handles appear side by side with the “Sponsored” label, while you control targeting, budget and measurement. Once the creator grants permission, you set them up in Ads Manager like any other ad. Below I explain what the format is good for, how to set it up and what to watch out for.

The format is back in the spotlight this month because Meta highlighted it in its Q3 2026 results. According to exchange4media, Meta CFO Susan Li said partnership ads grew more than 97% year over year and passed a $15 billion annual revenue run rate. That tells you advertisers are putting real money behind it; whether it works for your account is something only a test can answer.

How do Meta partnership ads work?

Instagram’s creator page says both the brand’s and the creator’s handle appear together with the “Sponsored” label, and the ad reaches beyond both accounts’ existing followers. Reels, stories, single-image posts and carousels all work, and depending on the brand’s campaign setup the ad can run on both Instagram and Facebook.

The difference from a classic influencer deal: the creator doesn’t just post and walk away. The same content runs as an ad in your account, and you decide who sees it, how much is spent and which conversion it optimizes for. Meta’s developer documentation describes the format as running Facebook and Instagram ads from a creator’s or partner’s handle to scale collaborations.

Do partnership ads really perform better?

Meta’s own numbers are positive, but you should confirm them with your own test. Meta data reported by eMarketer shows partnership ads delivering on average 19% lower CPA and 13% higher click-through rate than standard formats. PPC Land’s summary of Meta’s October 2026 creator report adds that across 20 conversion lift studies in EMEA, advertisers who gave partnership ads 30% or more of their campaigns saw a 45% lower median cost per incremental purchase and 35% higher median incremental ROAS.

Two caveats. All of these figures are Meta’s own measurements, not independently verified. And an average knows nothing about your product, price or the creator’s audience. I read them as “worth testing”, not as a guarantee.

How to set up a Meta partnership ad in 6 steps

Menu names change from time to time, but the flow usually looks like this:

  1. Check your measurement. If the ad optimizes for purchases, make sure the purchase event arrives complete. I cover the setup in my guide to Pixel and Conversions API; with broken tracking even the best content goes to the wrong people.
  2. Agree on permissions with the creator. The creator either posts with the paid partnership label and grants your brand ad permission, or sends you an ad code. Put the duration and the content covered into a written agreement.
  3. Open the campaign like a normal sales campaign. Set the objective, budget and audience as usual. Rather than a separate “influencer campaign” that splits your budget, adding partnership ads as new creatives to your existing sales campaign is usually more efficient. I compare structures in Advantage+ or manual campaign.
  4. Choose partnership ad at the ad level. When building the ad in Ads Manager, turn on the partnership ad option, pick the creator’s account or enter the ad code, then use the existing post or upload new content.
  5. Watch out for licensed music. Instagram says nearly all content is eligible as long as the ad permissions are in place and there’s no licensed music. A Reel cut to a popular song can cause trouble as an ad, so discuss it before filming.
  6. Add in batches and wait at least a week. Add new creatives together, not one by one, since each addition can restart learning. Then judge them on the same metrics as your brand creatives: cost per purchase, ROAS and click-through rate. I summarize those in which metrics to read in an ad report.

Which businesses is it right for?

Categories where the product is easy to show and try (cosmetics, apparel, home goods, food, accessories) are a natural fit. A local business doesn’t need a big influencer; a small but relevant account in its city can do the job. A café, for example, could run a video made with a local food account and show it only to people within 10 km.

The format is also a good answer to creative fatigue. When the same brand visuals run for weeks, performance drops; new faces and new storytelling bring a fresh angle. I explain when to refresh in my guide to ad fatigue.

What are the most common mistakes?

  • Choosing by follower count. What matters is that the content fits your target customer and looks like a moment of use, not a sales video.
  • Judging by one video. Test at least 3–4 different pieces; one video failing doesn’t mean the format failed.
  • Testing in a separate campaign with a tiny budget. The budget splits, the ad set never exits learning and the result misleads you.
  • Not putting the permission period in writing. A winning ad stops the day the permission expires.
  • Skipping disclosure. Meta’s “Sponsored” label marks the ad, but the creator’s own organic post should also disclose the partnership under the rules of your market.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Meta partnership ad?

An ad that runs a creator’s or partner’s content under their handle together with yours, from your ad account and on your budget. Both handles appear with the “Sponsored” label, and you keep control of targeting, budget and measurement.

What does the creator need to do?

The creator has to grant your brand ad permission, either by posting with the paid partnership label and allowing your brand to run it, or by sending you an ad code. Put the permission period in writing.

Does a partnership ad show on the creator’s profile?

It depends on the setup. According to Instagram, if the creator posted the content first and it was then turned into an ad, it shows on their grid; if it was uploaded directly through Ads Manager, it doesn’t.

Are partnership ads cheaper than regular ads?

Meta’s own data shows lower acquisition costs and higher click-through rates on average. These are Meta’s measurements, so test them in your own account alongside your brand creatives in the same campaign before deciding.
