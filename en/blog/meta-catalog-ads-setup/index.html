How to set up Meta catalog ads: a 6-step guide

By Yusuf Aslan · 7 min read · November 2026

Meta catalog ads (officially Advantage+ catalog ads) automatically pick products from your catalog and show each person the ones most relevant to them on Facebook and Instagram. To set them up you need four things: a catalog in Commerce Manager, a product feed that keeps it current, a Meta Pixel whose product IDs match the catalog exactly, and a sales campaign connected to that catalog. Below I walk through the setup in six steps, including how to connect common e-commerce platforms.

November is when catalog ads earn their keep. Around Black Friday, more visitors look at a product and leave without buying, and bringing them back with the exact product they viewed is far faster than designing ads one by one. If you are preparing the Google side at the same time, pair this guide with my Google Ads Black Friday preparation checklist.

What are Meta catalog ads, and how are they different from regular ads?

In a regular ad you design every image and line of copy. In a catalog ad you build a template, and the product name, price, image and link come from your feed. According to Meta’s Advantage+ catalog ads getting-started guide, fields like {{product.name}} or {{product.price}} are replaced with values from your data feed when the ad renders. With 500 products, you don’t make 500 ads; one setup covers them all.

The same guide describes three main ways to use catalog ads:

StrategyWho sees it?What it does
RetargetingPeople who viewed products but didn’t buy (Meta’s example: the last 3–5 days)Recovers sales left in the cart or on the product page
Cross-sell and upsellPeople who viewed or bought from one product setSuggests a complementary or higher-tier product
Broad audienceNew people defined by age, gender and locationShows relevant products to people who never visited your site

With a broad audience you hand most of the targeting to Meta. I discuss when that makes sense in Advantage+ or manual campaign.

What do you need before setting up catalog ads?

  • A Facebook Page for the business, plus an Instagram account if the ads will run there.
  • An ad account with a payment method on file.
  • A catalog created in Commerce Manager.
  • A Meta Pixel on your site sending ViewContent, AddToCart and Purchase events.

Skip the last one and retargeting won’t work, because Meta can’t tell who looked at which product. If your Pixel setup is shaky, start with the checks in setting up the Pixel and Conversions API; sending events the browser misses from your server also grows your catalog audiences.

How do you set up a Meta catalog ad in 6 steps?

  1. Create a catalog in Commerce Manager. Choose e-commerce as the catalog type.
  2. Connect products with a data feed. Use the feed URL from your platform instead of adding products by hand, so price and stock stay in sync.
  3. Connect the Pixel to the catalog. Check in Commerce Manager that the catalog recognizes the Pixel’s events as a data source. Meta’s Pixel documentation says this can take up to 24 hours to show up.
  4. Split the catalog into product sets. Build sets such as “women’s boots”, “on sale” or “best sellers” so you can leave low-margin products out of your ads.
  5. Launch a sales campaign and select the catalog. Choose which product set to promote at the ad set level.
  6. Set up the template and audiences, then watch for errors in week one. Use one ad set for viewers who didn’t buy and another for a broad audience, and check the Commerce Manager error report daily.

Which fields are required in the product feed?

Meta’s catalog fields reference requires these fields for every product:

FieldRuleTip
idUp to 100 characters, unique in the catalogUse your SKU if you can
titleUp to 200 characters65 recommended to avoid truncation
descriptionUp to 9,999 characters, plain textNo all-caps text or links
availabilityin stock / out of stockKeep stock data current
conditionnew / refurbished / used“new” for most stores
priceNumber + space + currency codeExample: 49.90 USD; period as decimal
linkStarts with http:// or https://Must point to a product page on your domain
image_linkJPEG or PNG, at least 500 × 500 px, up to 8 MBSquare images on a clean background look best
brandUp to 100 charactersYour brand or the manufacturer’s

The feed title appears right under the product in a catalog ad. Write one that fits in 65 characters and leads with the product type and its key feature; it is the same logic I explain in how to write a marketplace product title. Images matter just as much: small, cluttered or text-heavy photos look even worse in a catalog ad. My rules for product photos are in preparing product images and descriptions.

According to Meta’s catalog ads FAQ, a feed file uploaded through Business Manager can be up to 100 MB, in XML or tab-delimited CSV, TXT or TSV. Products marked out of stock stop showing automatically, and products with fatal errors are not uploaded at all.

How do you connect a catalog from Shopify or ikas?

On a store platform you rarely build the feed by hand. On Shopify, the Facebook & Instagram app does it. Shopify’s guide says products sync once you make them available to the Facebook channel, and edits usually reach Meta within a few minutes. To sync, a product needs a return policy, an assigned Google product category, and a title, description and image that meet Meta’s rules. If you want to check your store’s foundations too, my Shopify SEO settings checklist is a good start.

On ikas, the Turkish platform, its help page explains the flow: copy the catalog connection URL from Sales Channel > Extensions > Product Catalog Connections > Facebook Business Manager, create a catalog in Commerce Manager, choose “connect to a data feed”, paste the URL and pick your currency. ikas also recommends keeping the Pixel connected to ikas on and any other Pixels off. Other platforms follow the same pattern; if you can’t find the feed URL, ask your platform’s support for the “Meta catalog feed URL”.

Why is the Pixel–catalog match the most common error?

The problem I see most after setup: the ad is live but retargeting never works. The cause is usually an ID mismatch. Meta is explicit that the product ID the Pixel sends in content_ids must exactly match the id column in your feed. If the feed lists “SKU-1042” but the Pixel sends “1042”, Meta treats them as different products. The content_type value must also be “product” or “product_group”.

The check is simple: open a ViewContent event in Events Manager, note the ID it sent, and compare it with the same product’s id in Commerce Manager. Also watch audience size: Meta won’t deliver to an audience smaller than 20 people. If your traffic is low, use a longer lookback window instead of a short “last 3 days” audience.

What does the “product browsing” setting from October 2026 change?

A timely note: PPC Land reports that from October 28, 2026, Meta turned on “product browsing” by default for live ads. With it, someone who taps an ad may see product details and personalized suggestions inside the app before reaching your site. The report also notes Meta has not published a separate help page for the change; the setting can be switched on or off per ad under Advantage+ creative and destination settings.

My advice: don’t leave it on or off blindly going into Black Friday. In your highest-spending ad set, run two ads side by side, one with the setting on and one off, and compare sales on your site. Visitors who stay in the app may never fire a cart event on your site, which can shrink your retargeting audience.

How should you read catalog ad results?

Retargeting campaigns naturally show a high ROAS because the audience is already close to buying. So judge catalog ads alongside your broad campaigns and look at profit, not just the ratio; the metrics to read in an ad report help you keep that wider view.

Catalog ads get tired too. Showing the same product carousel to the same person for days pushes frequency up and clicks down. A seasonal frame or promo line on the template, and rotating product sets weekly, both help; the warning signs are in when to refresh a fatigued ad.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a website for Meta catalog ads?

Yes. Under Meta’s catalog rules, each product’s link must point to a product page on your own domain. Retargeting also needs a Meta Pixel on the site sending view, add-to-cart and purchase events.

Are dynamic ads and Advantage+ catalog ads the same thing?

Yes. The format once called dynamic product ads is now called Advantage+ catalog ads at Meta. The idea is the same: product data comes from the catalog and products are chosen per person.

Do out-of-stock products show in catalog ads?

No. Meta automatically stops showing products marked out of stock in the feed, which is why your feed needs current stock data.

Why isn’t my catalog ad delivering?

The usual causes: the product ID sent by the Pixel doesn’t match the feed id, the audience is smaller than 20 people, or products failed to upload because of feed errors. Check the Commerce Manager error report and Events Manager first.
