What is llms.txt? Should your online store add one?

By Yusuf Aslan · 6 min read · November 2026

llms.txt is a plain text file placed at the root of a website that summarises, in Markdown, what the site is and which pages matter most, so that large language models such as ChatGPT or Gemini can understand it quickly. It is a convention proposed in 2024, not an official standard, and Google states plainly that its Search results and AI features like AI Mode ignore it. Adding one to your online store won’t hurt, but it won’t move sales either; solid SEO fundamentals come first. Below I explain what the file does, who actually reads it and whether it makes sense for your store.

Lately I keep seeing packages sold with the promise that “you need llms.txt to show up in AI”. I wrote this to separate what is proven from what is a guess. If you are new to the idea, start with what GEO is and how to appear in AI answers.

What does an llms.txt file do?

The idea comes from a proposal Jeremy Howard of Answer.AI published on 3 September 2024. The reasoning is simple: AI systems reading a site get lost in HTML full of menus, cookie banners and scripts. llms.txt aims to give them a short, clean answer to “what is this site and which pages matter”. The proposal even gives an e-commerce example: stores could outline their product categories and policies such as returns and shipping.

The current version lives at llmstxt.org as “v2”. That August 2026 revision clarified how to link to Markdown copies of pages, added rules for separate llms.txt files in subfolders and turned the once-special “Optional” section into an ordinary heading.

How is llms.txt different from robots.txt and sitemap.xml?

All three are text files at the root of your domain, so they get mixed up, but they do different jobs:

FileWhat it doesWho reads it
robots.txtTells each bot which pages it may crawlSearch engine and AI crawlers (established standard)
sitemap.xmlLists the pages you want crawledSearch engines (established standard)
llms.txtGives a short Markdown overview and key pagesFew systems; Google Search does not use it

The key difference: robots.txt is a permissions file, llms.txt is an introduction. Adding llms.txt neither blocks nor invites any bot. If you want to control AI crawler access, robots.txt is still where you look; it is the last item on my SEO and GEO checklist.

Does Google use llms.txt?

No. Google’s guide to optimising for generative AI features, published in May 2026, says you don’t need to create new machine-readable files, AI text files or Markdown to appear in Google Search; because Search ignores them, they neither help nor harm your visibility or rankings. The same guide says that to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, and that SEO best practices still apply.

Google’s John Mueller said something similar much earlier. In a June 2025 post he wrote that no AI system currently uses llms.txt. In June 2026, on the Search Off the Record podcast, he explained the core weakness: because site owners write the file themselves, an AI can’t trust it to tell sites apart. The one scenario he left open was automated systems helping someone who is already on your site (Search Engine Journal’s report).

If you want to know how your store will appear in Google’s AI search, the steps that actually matter are in what to change on an online store for Google AI Mode.

So why does Chrome audit llms.txt?

This is the confusing part. In 2026 Chrome’s Lighthouse audit tool gained an “Agentic Browsing” category, and it includes an llms.txt audit. Chrome’s documentation says that without the file, agents may spend more time crawling to understand a site’s structure. But it adds two important notes: if the file is missing (a 404), the audit is marked not applicable because the file is optional for now, and a page is only flagged when fetching the file returns a server error.

Search Engine Journal summed it up as Google’s Search and Chrome documentation pointing in different directions. My reading: for Google Search, llms.txt has no value; for AI shopping agents, it is an unsettled, experimental area.

Should you add llms.txt to your online store?

My short answer: there’s no rush, but it is a fifteen-minute job, so adding one does no harm. Ask yourself:

  • Are your product pages indexed in Google? If not, spend that time on indexing problems instead.
  • Is your product data marked up with structured data? That is where machines read price, stock and reviews; I cover the setup in structured data (schema) on product pages.
  • Does your platform let you put a file at the root? Hosted platforms don’t always allow it, and it isn’t worth forcing.
  • Will you keep it up to date? An llms.txt full of expired campaigns and closed categories is no better than none.

How do you create an llms.txt file? 5 steps

  1. Write the title. Per llmstxt.org, the only required section is an H1 with the brand or site name (a line starting with #).
  2. Add a one-line summary. Under the title, add a quote line starting with “>”: what you sell, to whom and where you ship.
  3. List your key pages. Under H2 headings such as “## Categories” and “## Policies”, use one [page name](URL): short note per line. List main categories and your returns, shipping and contact pages, not every product.
  4. Upload it to the root. The address should be yourdomain.com/llms.txt and it should open as plain text.
  5. Check it doesn’t return a server error. Open it in a browser; a server error is the only thing Lighthouse flags.

A short example for a store (fictional brand):

# Example Store

> Online store selling handmade ceramic mugs and plates, shipping across Europe.

## Categories
- [Mugs](https://examplestore.com/mugs): Handmade ceramic mugs
- [Plates](https://examplestore.com/plates): Serving and dinner plates

## Policies
- [Returns](https://examplestore.com/returns): 14-day return conditions
- [Shipping](https://examplestore.com/shipping): Delivery times and costs

What should you prioritise instead of llms.txt?

What decides visibility in AI answers is still your pages themselves. Google’s guide points the same way: crawlable, indexed pages that give people clear information. My order of work:

  1. Indexed, fast product and category pages, especially unique copy on category pages.
  2. Product structured data that matches what the page shows, exactly.
  3. Content that answers the question in its first sentence, with a date and a named author; I explain how in how to write content that AI will quote.
  4. Once all that is in place, llms.txt as a low-cost extra while you watch how AI agents develop.

Frequently asked questions

Does llms.txt affect SEO?

Not for Google Search. Google’s guide to generative AI features says Search ignores AI text files, so llms.txt neither helps nor harms your rankings or your visibility in AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Does ChatGPT read llms.txt?

There is no official confirmation. Google’s John Mueller wrote in June 2025 that no AI system currently uses llms.txt. Who reads the file is still an open question, so don’t invest in it on a promise of visibility.

Is llms.txt the same as robots.txt?

No. robots.txt is a permissions file that tells bots which pages they may crawl. llms.txt is an introduction that offers a short Markdown overview of the site and its key pages; it neither blocks nor invites any bot.

Where does the llms.txt file go?

At the root of your site, so it opens at yourdomain.com/llms.txt. The current proposal on llmstxt.org also allows separate files in subfolders; for pages in a folder, the most specific file applies.

Does my online store need llms.txt?

No, it is optional. Even Chrome’s Lighthouse audit doesn’t treat a missing file as an error. Focus first on indexed product pages, structured data and clear content; you can add llms.txt later as a low-cost extra.
