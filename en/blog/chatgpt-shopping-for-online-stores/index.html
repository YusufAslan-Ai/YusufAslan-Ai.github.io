ChatGPT shopping: how your products show up in ChatGPT

By Yusuf Aslan · 8 min read · November 2026

ChatGPT shopping is the feature where ChatGPT answers a “what should I buy” question with product cards, prices and links to sellers. According to OpenAI, these product results are not ads: ChatGPT picks products independently based on what the user is asking, and ranks sellers by availability, price, quality and whether they are the maker or primary seller of the item. Shopping results are currently live only for users in the U.S., but the groundwork a store outside the U.S. can do today rests on the same foundations as classic SEO. Below I explain how the system works and what to fix on your side.

A growing share of shoppers now start product research in ChatGPT rather than Google. Ask it for “the best coffee grinder under $50” and it searches the web and shows a handful of products and sources. I covered the general idea of being cited in AI answers in what GEO is and how to appear in AI answers. This article is the hands-on follow-up for products and stores.

How do ChatGPT shopping results work?

OpenAI’s help page on shopping with ChatGPT makes three points that matter:

  • Results are not ads. ChatGPT selects product results independently; OpenAI partnerships do not influence them, and ads are kept separate.
  • A product appears when it is relevant. The way into the carousel is not a bid, it is ChatGPT being able to match your product to the user’s intent.
  • The data comes from structured sources. Details such as price and product description are read from structured metadata supplied by first-party and third-party providers.

The same page says that when several merchants sell the same item, they are ranked on factors like availability, price, quality and whether they are the maker or primary seller. That last factor matters if you sell your own brand: selling it on your own site gives you a natural edge over resellers of the same product.

Does the purchase happen inside ChatGPT?

Not any more. In 2025 OpenAI announced Instant Checkout for paying inside ChatGPT. In its product discovery update of March 24, 2026, it said that first version lacked flexibility, and that it now lets merchants use their own checkout while it focuses on discovery. The ChatGPT merchants page says the same: users discover and compare products in ChatGPT, and complete the purchase on the merchant’s own website or app.

In practice, a visitor from ChatGPT lands on your product page. Page speed, clear pricing, shipping and returns information still decide the sale. I collected the points I check to turn that visitor into a buyer in my guide on how to increase e-commerce conversion rate.

What does this mean for stores outside the U.S.?

According to OpenAI’s merchants page, shopping in ChatGPT is currently live for users in the U.S. and will expand to more regions over time, with no dates given for other countries such as Turkey. Merchants from other countries can apply through the merchant form and join a waitlist.

How do you get your products shown in ChatGPT? 6 steps

Even where the shopping carousel is not live yet, people ask ChatGPT about products and it searches the web and cites sources. These steps help you get cited today and be ready when shopping reaches your market:

  1. Do not block OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI’s crawler documentation says OAI-SearchBot is used to surface websites in ChatGPT search results, and sites that opt out of it are not shown in ChatGPT search answers. A robots.txt change can take around 24 hours to take effect. GPTBot, which crawls for model training, is a separate bot; blocking it does not affect your search visibility.
  2. Check your CDN and firewall. Some platform and CDN settings block bot traffic wholesale. If the server turns the bot away, robots.txt permission does not help. Ask your host or platform support whether OpenAI’s bots are blocked.
  3. Add structured data to product pages. OpenAI says it reads price and description from structured metadata. Your Product schema should include price, currency, availability and review data; I walk through it in structured data (schema) on product pages.
  4. Keep price and stock current. Availability and price are named ranking factors. A product marked “in stock” that says “sold out” in the cart sends the wrong signal to ChatGPT and to the shopper.
  5. Write product copy that answers questions. Size, material, who it suits, shipping and return times should be on the page as text, not baked into images. ChatGPT shows a product when it finds it relevant to the user’s intent, and text is what builds that match. I show how in how to write content that AI will quote.
  6. Make your brand and seller status clear. If you make your own products, show the brand, contact and company details on the site. Remember OpenAI says it looks at the “maker or primary seller” signal.

Do you need llms.txt or a special file?

OpenAI’s shopping and merchant pages do not mention llms.txt; they talk about structured data, product feeds and bot access. The merchants FAQ is clear on feeds, too: if ChatGPT already crawls your site, a feed is not required, but it gives you more control over how your products appear. I gave my view on the file in what llms.txt is and whether your store should add one.

How do you measure traffic from ChatGPT?

Open the traffic acquisition report in Analytics and look for chatgpt.com among session sources; visitors who click a link in ChatGPT usually show up there. I recommend defining it as its own channel group so its contribution to sales does not blend into Google organic. If the number is small, do not worry; what matters is which product pages receive it. Those pages show you which questions ChatGPT matches your products to.

Frequently asked questions

Are ChatGPT shopping results ads?

No. OpenAI’s help page says ChatGPT selects product results independently; they are not ads and are not influenced by OpenAI partnerships. Ads are shown separately from product results.

Is ChatGPT shopping available outside the U.S.?

According to OpenAI’s merchants page, shopping is currently live for users in the U.S. and will expand to other regions over time. No dates have been given for other countries. ChatGPT search can still cite websites as sources everywhere.

Do I have to pay for my products to appear in ChatGPT?

No. Product results are not paid placements. OpenAI’s merchants page also says there are no fees on purchases that start in ChatGPT.

What happens if I block OAI-SearchBot?

According to OpenAI’s crawler documentation, sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot are not shown in ChatGPT search answers. GPTBot, which crawls for model training, is a separate bot; blocking it does not affect search visibility.

If I use Shopify or Etsy, do I need to apply separately?

OpenAI says Shopify and Etsy catalogs are already integrated into ChatGPT, with no extra setup or application. On other platforms, direct product feeds go through OpenAI’s merchant application, which currently has a waitlist.
