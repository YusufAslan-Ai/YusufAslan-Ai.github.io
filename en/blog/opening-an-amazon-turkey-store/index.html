How to open an Amazon Turkey store: a 2026 guide

By Yusuf Aslan · 7 min read · November 2026

To open a store on Amazon Turkey (Amazon.com.tr) you need a tax-registered business (a sole proprietorship, limited or joint-stock company), then you sign up through Seller Central and verify your identity, company, address and credit card; nothing is charged to the card at sign-up. The timing is good this year too: sellers who open their first account between 30 September and 31 December 2026 pay a 1% referral fee for 90 days or up to 2 million TL in sales. Below I cover the documents to prepare, the sign-up steps and how to use the offer without hurting your margins.

Most businesses already selling on Trendyol or Hepsiburada keep Amazon on their “later” list. But Amazon’s catalogue, fulfilment service and ad system work differently from the other Turkish marketplaces, and knowing those differences up front saves you months of trial and error. If you want to compare the process, see my step-by-step guide to opening a Hepsiburada store.

Who can sell on Amazon Turkey?

The FAQ on Amazon Turkey’s seller page is clear: to sell on Amazon.com.tr you must be a taxpayer with a legally established business. Sole proprietorships, limited and joint-stock companies all qualify. Amazon’s seller blog adds that every sale needs a legal invoice and that second-hand goods can’t be sold on Amazon Turkey.

The blog also mentions a free “Individual” plan for people selling fewer than 40 items a month. Because the seller page requires tax registration and every sale needs an invoice, I don’t recommend that route to anyone without a company; check your own situation in Seller Central before you apply.

Which documents do you need?

According to Amazon’s getting started guide and seller page, have these ready before you start:

Document / detailNote
Business e-mail and phonePhone number with country code; a verification code is sent
IDValid national ID card or passport of the primary contact
Company detailsBusiness type, registry number, tax number, registered address; tax certificate and trade registry gazette or chamber record
Proof of addressCredit card statement or bank receipt; the blog says it must be issued within the last 180 days
Credit cardPersonal or company card; Amazon says nothing is charged at sign-up
Bank accountWhere your sales payouts are deposited

The single most important thing is that the company name and address match exactly across every document. If your card statement shows a different address from your tax certificate, fix that before you start.

How do you open the store, step by step?

  1. Create a Seller Central account. Sign up with your e-mail and verify with the code sent to your phone.
  2. Enter your business details. Type the company type, name, tax and registry details exactly as they appear on your documents.
  3. Add seller, billing and payment details. Add your credit card and the bank account for payouts.
  4. Set up your store. Choose a store name that matches your brand and is easy to remember.
  5. Complete identity verification. Amazon’s blog says a video call with an Amazon representative may be requested; keep your original documents at hand.
  6. Wait for the review. Amazon doesn’t publish a fixed approval time; once the account is active you can list products.

Once you’re in, getting the catalogue data, title and images right decides how you rank. I collected the checklist in getting your first product ready to sell on Amazon.

Is it free, and how much is the commission?

The getting started guide lists the Professional plan at 99 TL + VAT a month, and says new sign-ups are exempt from it as a limited-time offer. The real cost is the referral fee, a category-based share of each sale. Some of the current standard rates listed on Amazon’s campaign page:

CategoryStandard referral fee
Clothing15.5%
Shoes17%
Furniture14.5%
Toys13%
Books10.2%
Electronics9.5%
Computers7%
Other10%

In jewellery, personal care and grocery the rate depends on the item price, so check your own category in Seller Central before pricing. Amazon pays out every 14 days, after fees. If you use Fulfilment by Amazon (FBA), add a per-unit fulfilment fee and a monthly storage fee based on volume.

How does the 1% commission offer work?

Per the campaign page: sellers who open their first seller account between 30 September and 31 December 2026 and list at least one product within 20 days pay a 1% referral fee for 90 days or until they reach 2,000,000 TL in sales, whichever comes first. Mobile phones, computers, electronics and electronics accessories are excluded. Sellers who have never used Sponsored Products also get a 4,000 TL ad credit with the promo code on the campaign page; you need to start a campaign within 14 days of entering the code and use the credit within 30 days.

In numbers (the price is hypothetical): on a 600 TL clothing item, the standard 15.5% fee is 93 TL; at 1% it drops to 6 TL. That 87 TL per unit gives you real room to price more aggressively or fund ads in the first 90 days. The catch: after 90 days you go back to the standard rate. If you set prices around the campaign fee, you can slide into a loss in month four. Work out your break-even price at the standard rate first and treat the discount as a temporary buffer. What should you focus on in the first 90 days?

  • Prepare products before you sign up. Twenty days to list is short; have barcodes, titles and images ready beforehand.
  • Hit the performance targets from day one. The getting started guide says order defect rate should stay under 1%, pre-fulfilment cancellations under 2.5% and late shipments under 4%. Don’t list items you don’t have in stock.
  • Spend the ad credit on profitable products. Give the 4,000 TL to your 3–5 best-margin products instead of the whole catalogue. The budgeting logic is the same as in my guide on how to use marketplace ads.
  • Plan channels together. If you sell handmade or personalised items, Etsy is another way abroad; compare the fees with the worked example in Etsy fees per sale.

Frequently asked questions

Is it free to open an Amazon Turkey store?

Nothing is charged to your card at sign-up. The Professional plan costs 99 TL + VAT a month, but Amazon currently exempts new sign-ups as a limited-time offer. The main cost is the category-based referral fee on each sale.

Can I sell on Amazon Turkey without a company?

Amazon Turkey’s seller page requires sellers to be taxpayers with a legally established business, and each sale needs a legal invoice. So you should set up a sole proprietorship or limited company first.

Who qualifies for Amazon’s 1% commission offer?

First-time sellers who open an account between 30 September and 31 December 2026 and list a product within 20 days. The 1% rate lasts 90 days or up to 2 million TL in sales; mobile phones, computers, electronics and electronics accessories are excluded.

How long does Amazon seller approval take?

Amazon doesn’t publish a fixed time. It depends on consistent documents and identity verification, which may include a video call. Matching company name and address on every document reduces delays.

When does Amazon pay sellers?

According to Amazon Turkey’s seller page, payouts are made every 14 days to your registered bank account, after referral and other fees are deducted.
