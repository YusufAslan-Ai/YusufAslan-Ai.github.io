Google AI Mode for e-commerce: what should you change?

By Yusuf Aslan · 6 min read · November 2026

Google AI Mode is a search experience where you ask a question conversationally, dig deeper with follow-up questions and get an answer that links out to source websites. Google began rolling out AI Mode, along with the AI Overviews summaries that appear at the top of results, in Türkiye on 18 February 2026. According to Google, there are no extra technical requirements or special markup for an online store to appear in these features: the page needs to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. The rest is solid SEO, clear content and up-to-date product data. Below I explain what this means for your store and which steps to take first, based on Google’s own documentation.

I covered the general idea of visibility in AI answers in what GEO is and how to appear in AI answers. This article is the hands-on follow-up, specific to Google and e-commerce.

What is the difference between AI Mode and AI Overviews?

Both run on the Gemini 3 model family and both show source links inside the answer. The difference is in how people search. Based on Google’s Türkiye announcement (in Turkish), here is the short version:

AI OverviewsAI Mode
Where does it appear?At the top of regular results, when Google decides it helpsA separate search mode in the Google app (Android, iOS) and on the results page
What does it do?Gives the core answer as a short summaryHandles complex questions and goes deeper with follow-ups
How do people ask?The classic search boxText, voice or an image from the camera
Links to sites?Source links inside the summaryWeb links in and next to the answer
Ads?Ads continue in dedicated spaces above and below the summaryNot specified in the announcement

The key point for e-commerce: instead of typing “black leather backpack”, a shopper can now ask “which water-resistant backpack under 3,000 TRY fits a 15-inch laptop for my daily commute?”. The answer is assembled from many pages, not one ranked list.

How does AI Mode build its answers?

Google calls the technique “query fan-out”: AI Mode breaks a complex question into subtopics and runs many searches at once. According to Google Search Central’s page on AI features, this lets it show a wider and more diverse set of links than classic search.

In practice, ranking first for one head keyword is no longer enough on its own. In the backpack example, AI Mode may search separately for laptop sleeve size, water resistance and price range. If your product and category pages answer each of those sub-questions clearly, your chances of being linked go up.

Do you need special optimization for AI Mode?

No. Google says it plainly: to be shown as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet, and there are no additional technical requirements. The same page states there is no special schema.org structured data you need to add for these features.

So don’t pay anyone for “AI Mode code”. Structured data still matters, just not as something new: Google wants the markup to match the visible text on the page. I explain how to mark up price, availability and reviews correctly in the guide to structured data on product pages.

Which steps should an online store take?

Adapting Google’s recommendations for succeeding in AI search to an online store, this is the order I would follow:

  1. Remove crawling and indexing blockers. Use the URL Inspection tool in Search Console to confirm Googlebot isn’t blocked in robots.txt and important pages return a successful status code. Per Google, the Google-Extended setting governs training and grounding in other Google systems, not visibility in Search, so don’t mix the two up.
  2. Don’t switch off snippets by accident. nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex limit how your content shows in these features. If a theme or plugin has added them to product descriptions, the page can’t even appear as a link.
  3. Write original copy for category pages. Add a short intro that answers sub-questions, a buying guide and the logic behind your filters. I walk through it in category pages and e-commerce SEO.
  4. Answer real questions on product pages. Put size, material, use case, shipping and return times in text, not inside images. Google stresses unique, non-commodity content; copying the manufacturer’s text is the opposite. For sentence-level tips, see how to write content that AI will quote.
  5. Keep Merchant Center and your Business Profile up to date. Google lists both among its recommendations for AI search. If your feed has disapproved products, fix those first using the Merchant Center disapproval fix guide. If your product feed already powers Shopping ads, the work pays off twice; I cover the feed–campaign link in Performance Max versus Shopping campaigns.
  6. Add high-quality images and video. AI Mode lets people search with their camera, and Google recommends supporting text with good images and videos for multimodal search.
  7. Check page experience and mobile speed. A visitor arriving from an AI answer expects a fast page with clearly separated main content, just like one from classic search.

If you run Shopify, pair these steps with the Shopify SEO settings checklist. To tick everything off on one page, use the 10-point checklist for SEO and GEO.

How do you measure traffic from AI Mode?

According to Google, traffic from AI Overviews and AI Mode is counted inside the “Web” search type in the Search Console Performance report, not as a separate line. So track the change through conversions rather than click counts.

Google’s own guidance points the same way: it says clicks from results pages with AI Overviews tend to be higher quality, with users more likely to spend more time on the site, and it advises looking at conversions such as sales and sign-ups instead of raw clicks. That only works if measurement is solid; to compare organic and paid traffic on the same conversion definition, apply the checks in setting up Google Ads conversion tracking correctly.

Are the new AI reports in Merchant Center available in Türkiye?

MarTech reported in May 2026 that Google is adding reports to Merchant Center on product performance in AI-powered shopping experiences (Search, Gemini and AI Overviews): share of voice against similar brands, funnel performance from discovery to purchase, the conversational queries shoppers use, and missing product attributes such as color, material and style. The first rollout covers the US, Canada, Australia, India and New Zealand; Türkiye isn’t on the list.

You don’t need to wait for the report to fix missing attributes: list the products in your feed with empty color, material, size or product type fields and fill them in.

What should you avoid?

  • Don’t block bots in a panic. Block Googlebot and you drop out of classic search too.
  • Don’t mass-produce copy written only for AI. Google’s advice is the opposite: content that adds original value for people.
  • Don’t put information in schema that isn’t on the page. Showing 1,299 TRY on the page and 999 TRY in the markup costs you trust.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google AI Mode available in Türkiye?

Yes. Google began gradually rolling out AI Mode and AI Overviews in Türkiye on 18 February 2026. They appear in the Google app on Android and iOS and on the search results page.

Do I need special code or schema for AI Mode?

No. According to Google, a page only needs to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet; there are no additional technical requirements and no special schema.org markup. What matters is that your existing structured data matches the visible content.

Can I see AI Mode visitors in Search Console?

Not as a separate line. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic is counted inside the “Web” search type in the Performance report. Tracking conversions such as sales and sign-ups gives a clearer picture than clicks.

Can I keep my site out of AI Mode?

Yes. nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex let you limit how your content appears in these features. These controls also affect how you appear in classic results, so online stores rarely use them.
